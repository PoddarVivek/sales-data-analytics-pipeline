# Findings

Generated by `pipeline.py` from `data/retail_sales.csv`. Re-run the pipeline and every figure below updates.

## Data quality
- 2000 rows received, 3 dropped for missing money fields, 1997 analysed.
- 10 kept rows have no age; they are ignored in age analysis only.
- No duplicate transaction IDs, and total_sale equals quantity x price on every row.

## What the data says
1. **Sales are strongly seasonal.** September to December brings 57% of two-year revenue in 8 of 24 months. An average peak month earns 65,440 against 24,262 in the other months (2.7x).
2. **The peak is driven by order volume, not basket size.** Average order value is 461 in peak months and 454 otherwise, so stock and staffing matter more than pricing in Q4.
3. **Categories are nearly interchangeable.** Revenue differs by only 9% between the best and worst category, and margins sit within about one point of each other.
4. **Evenings dominate.** 64% of orders are placed from 5 pm.
5. **Revenue is spread across customers.** The top 10% of customers (15 of 155) account for 30.3% of revenue, so there is no single-customer dependency.
6. **Younger buyers spend more per order.** Average order value falls from 504 (18-24) to 418 (55+).

## Caveats worth stating
- Customers 3, 1, 5, 2, 4 place 63 to 76 orders each, far above the average of about 13. Sequential low IDs with heavy activity can indicate placeholder or shared accounts, so customer-level conclusions need checking.
- Equal margins across categories and very regular order values suggest this dataset is synthetic. Treat the patterns as a demonstration of method, not as market evidence.
- Two years of data cannot separate seasonality from one-off promotions.
